About Me

A multi-layered story of passion, purpose, and the pursuit of human-centered technology

🧭

From Psychology to Code

My path to technology was not a straight line. I began in psychology, drawn to how people think, decide, and interact with the world around them. Over time, I realized I wanted to move from studying how people interact with the world to creating the things they interact with, the things that make life easier and better. That realization led me to computer science, where I now bring an understanding of human behavior into engineering practice.

At the University of South Florida, I am pursuing dual degrees in Computer Science and Psychology, combining analytical problem solving with a deep understanding of people. My research through the National Science Foundation explored how human decision making connects with machine learning systems, and my software projects focus on building tools that enhance accessibility, usability, and everyday efficiency.

I am most interested in how technology can understand and adapt to human needs. Whether I am developing code, designing interfaces, or analyzing data, my goal remains constant: to create designs that work with people, not just for them.

Research

Advancing ML Security & Human–Computer Interaction

Research Overview

In 2025, I was selected for the National Science Foundation research internship program and completed my site placement at the University of Alabama. I worked alongside doctorate-level researchers, PhD students, and other undergraduates on a project at the intersection of machine learning security and human psychology.

My research focused on how adversarial attacks exploit both technical weaknesses in autonomous vehicle models and the cognitive biases people rely on when reacting to these systems. The goal was to better understand the human-computer interaction behind AV decision-making and explore ways to design AI that accounts for human behavior instead of ignoring it.

Using Unity in C# and virtual reality, I created adversarial driving scenarios that captured biometric data such as pupil dilation, fixation duration, and saccade amplitude. This work offered insight into how humans perceive and respond to corrupted or misleading machine learning outputs, and how models can be made more resilient by considering these responses.

GreenLight Eats — Restaurant Safety App — In Progress

Using data to make dining safer, simpler, and more personal.

Description:
GreenLight Eats is a restaurant safety app designed to help users make informed dining choices through visual and data-driven insights. Built with React, TypeScript, Tailwind, and Vite, and prototyped in Figma, the app analyzes restaurant menus for allergens, dietary restrictions, and nutritional factors. Each menu item receives a dynamic safety score visualized through interactive Recharts components such as color-coded donuts and badges, allowing users to instantly see how well a dish aligns with their health, allergy, or religious needs. The ongoing prototype focuses on clarity and accessibility, translating nutritional data into intuitive visuals. GreenLight Eats aims to merge health awareness with technology, turning complex food data into confident and inclusive dining decisions.
